# Charlie Parker Omnibook (C edition): original recordings behind each solo

Compiled 2026-10-23 from web sources: jazzdisco.org, Peter Losin's *Miles Ahead* session database, Wikipedia, microgroove.jp (Mercury/Clef), Discogs, allsolos.com, and others listed in the source key below.

## How to read the table

- **Parker (as) plays on every row**, so personnel lists only the other musicians, pianist first.
- **Take**: the take that the Omnibook's printed label reference points to. When an LP held more than one take, I worked out which take the cited LPs have in common; those rows say "inferred".
- **Take numbers** differ between discographies. Losin and the Complete Savoy & Dial box count false starts. jazzdisco uses Savoy's LP labels ("orig.", "new", "short"). The Verve box prints both numbers, e.g. "Take 11 (Take 6)". Every master named below is the side that was actually issued on 78 rpm or 10-inch LP.
- **Key**: Omnibook concert key, taken from the iReal Pro forum set of the Hal Leonard Omnibook charts in concert key [IREAL]. For Savoy and Dial studio masters it is cross-checked with allsolos.com [AS].
- **Form**: filled in only where a source states it. Otherwise it is marked *(unconfirmed)* with my best reading.
- **Confidence**: High means two or more agreeing sources. Med means a single source or an inference. Low means it could not be determined.

### 2. Omnibook solos that are not the familiar studio masters

- **Anthropology** (p. 10) comes from the **Birdland broadcast of 31 Mar 1951** with Gillespie and **Bud Powell**. It is not the 1945 "Thriving from a Riff", which the Omnibook prints separately on p. 60.
- **Cheryl, Barbados and Chasing the Bird** (pp. 58, 70, 82) are cited to SJL 1108, so they are **Royal Roost broadcasts from 1949**, with **Al Haig** on piano and Kenny Dorham on trumpet. They are not the 1947 or 1948 studio takes with Powell or Lewis.
  - Savoy's 1977 track notes and jazzdisco agree that SJL 1108 holds these titles, and the LP is dated Feb–Mar 1949.
  - The per-title dates (Barbados 12 Feb; Cheryl and Chasin' the Bird 12 Mar 1949) come from jazzdisco alone.
  - Several Royal Roost performances of each tune exist, so this identification rests on the Omnibook's catalog reference. It should be confirmed by ear against the SJL 1108 or MG 12179 tracks.
- **Shawnuff** (p. 128) is cited to Phoenix 17, so it is the **AFRS Jubilee broadcast from Hollywood, late 1945** (Haig, Ray Brown, Stan Levey). It is not the May 1945 Guild record with Catlett.
  - jazzdisco's Parker and Haig pages list no LP issue for this Shaw 'Nuff take (JUB162), while Discogs puts Shaw 'Nuff at A1 of Phoenix LP 17. Medium confidence.
- **Celerity** (p. 22) was issued as **"Celebrity"**, a Hank Jones / Ray Brown / Buddy Rich quartet track from autumn (October) 1950. It was linked to the Granz/Mili film *Improvisation*.
- **Blues (Fast)** (p. 124) is a **quartet studio date from March 1950** with Hank Jones, Ray Brown and Buddy Rich, first issued in 1957 on *Jazz Perennial*. It is not a jam session or broadcast.
- **Visa and Passport** are Mercury/Clef studio sides from 1949 with Dorham and Haig.
  - **Two different tunes are called "Passport"** [KS, MG-512, JD-V49, WP-BOX "Tune Y (rare)" and "Tune Z (common)"].
  - The Omnibook cites Verve 8000 and 8009, which carry only the **"common" take, C 295-5**. Kevin Sun, citing Schaap, says the Omnibook's tune is the rhythm-changes "Passport", and that the rare one is a C blues. So the Omnibook = 295-5.

### 3. Takes inferred from the overlap of the cited LPs (medium confidence)

- **Chi Chi**: the Omnibook cites 8005, MGM 4949 and **8409**. Of the issued takes, only **1246-3 (an alternate)** is on both 8005 and 8409. The master, 1246-6 (Clef 89138), is not on 8409.
- **Cosmic Rays**: the Omnibook cites **8840** and 8005. 8840 has only **1121-5**. The master 1121-2 (Clef 89129) is on 8005 but not 8840, so the Omnibook solo is probably **1121-5, an alternate**.
  - This relies on jazzdisco's listing of 8840's contents.
- **Leap Frog, An Oscar for Treadwell and Back Home Blues**: only the master take is common to every cited LP, so these are the masters.
  - Leap Frog: 414-6, which the Verve box calls "Take 11 (Take 6)".
  - An Oscar for Treadwell: 411-4.
  - Back Home Blues: 612-2.
  - Same-title question: all eleven Leap Frog takes come from the one session on 6 Jun 1950, and no other Leap Frog session was found. The cited Verve 8002 and 8840 contain only 414-6.
- **Kim No. 1/No. 2, Mohawk No. 1/No. 2 and Au Privave No. 1/No. 2**: each pair is the two issued takes of that tune (Kim 1120-4 and 1120-2; Mohawk 412-6 and 412-3; Au Privave 489-3 and 489-2). The label references cannot tell which page is which take; that needs a listening check.

### 4. Date discrepancies

- **July 1953 quartet** (Chi Chi, Now's the Time No. 1): jazzdisco and Wikipedia give **28 Jul 1953**; Fresh Sound gives **30 Jul 1953**.
- **Visa**: jazzdisco and microgroove give Feb–Mar 1949. Wikipedia's *Bird and Diz* table gives 3 Feb 1949, but its own text says March.
- **Celebrity**: "Oct 1950" (jazzdisco) versus "autumn 1950" (Fresh Sound).
- **Shaw 'Nuff (Jubilee)**: jazzdisco says "possibly" 29 Dec 1945.
- **Studio names**: "Harry Smith Studios" (jazzdisco) and "Harris Smith Studio" (Losin, Wikipedia) are the same place. For Merry-Go-Round, allsolos gives 18 Sep 1948, which is wrong; jazzdisco, Losin and Wikipedia all give 24 Sep 1948.

### 5. Personnel cautions

- **Nov 26, 1945 piano**: Gillespie plays on Billie's Bounce and Now's the Time. Sadik Hakim plays on Thriving on a Riff (jazzdisco, Wikipedia). Losin cites Urbano's view that Gillespie plays only the intro of Thriving take 1.
- **Wikipedia errors**: the "Anthropology (composition)" article wrongly adds Milt Jackson to the 1945 date. For the 1953 tracks, Wikipedia's *Now's the Time* album page, jazzdisco and Fresh Sound all give Al Haig and Percy Heath.
- **Moose the Mooche**: only jazzdisco says Arv Garrison is out on this title (it marks him "#4-11").
